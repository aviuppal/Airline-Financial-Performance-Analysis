# Airline Financial Performance Analysis

### FirstByte 2026 Case Competition | Python · pandas · NumPy · Matplotlib

## Overview
An exploratory analysis of airline financial performance across two fiscal years, investigating revenue trends, operating costs, and other factors contributing to rising expenses.

Using over 559,000 customer transactions alongside operating cost and airport lease datasets, I performed data cleaning, validation, and EDA to identify fiscan trends and investigate potential cost drivers.

## Business Questions
- How did revenue and transaction volume change between FY24-25 and FY25–26?
- How did operating costs change relative to revenue?
- Which expense categories contributed most to rising operating costs?
- What relationships exist between flight volume and operating expenses?

## Tools & Technologies
- **Python:** Data processing & analysis
- **pandas:** Data cleaning, transformation, aggregation
- **NumPy:** Numerical operations
- **Matplotlib:** Data visualization

## Methodology
1. **Data Cleaning & Validation:** Investigated duplicate records, inconsistent formats, and missing values. Developed cleaning and validation steps to improve data consistency.
2. **Exploratory Data Analysis:** Examined transaction patterns, revenue, operating expenses, and airport lease costs.
3. **Financial Analysis:** Compared financial performance across FY24–25 and FY25–26, investigating changes in revenue, transaction volume, and operating costs.
4. **Data Visualization:** Created visualizations to communicate financial trends, expense breakdowns, and potential contributors to rising costs.

## Key Findings

| Metric | FY24–25 | FY25–26 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $180.3M | $189.5M | +5.1% |
| Transactions | 269,664 | 287,382 | +6.6% |
| Operating Costs | $193.0M | $238.4M | +23.5% |

**Key observation:** Operating costs increased faster than revenue between the two fiscal years. These findings demonstrate the importance of investigating individual expense categories and operational factors instead of evaluating financial performance through revenue growth alone.
